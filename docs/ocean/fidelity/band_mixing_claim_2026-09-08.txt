THE CLAIM: our turbulent mixing collapses below 20 m. At 20-60 m we mix heat at ~1% of the oracle's rate and momentum at ~5%, and our Prandtl number is 6.9 where the oracle's is 1.15. This is the largest discrepancy the campaign has measured and it sits exactly where our profile fails (0.5-1 K too warm at 0.5 m, 1.3 K too cold at 20 m).

WHAT IT IS BEING USED FOR: a one-variable 30-day arm is queued that flips our TKE mixing-length choice from 3 to 4, which is the oracle's own nn_mxl=2. Pre-registered primary: the 20-60 m tracer ratio rises from 0.009 to >= 0.2; below 0.05 the lever is refuted.

WHERE I THINK THIS IS MOST LIKELY TO BE WRONG, attack these first:
1. ARE THE TWO QUANTITIES THE SAME? Ours is K_H_diag / K_M_diag saved from the snapshot -- what the implicit vertical solve consumes. NEMO's is avt / avm on its w-points. Same definition, same placement, same inclusion of background and of any EVD contribution?
2. IS A MEDIAN THE RIGHT REDUCTION? Our K is one instantaneous field; NEMO's is a daily mean over hourly output. Where mixing is intermittent a median of means is not a mean of medians, and the 0-20 m row already shows NEMO's daily mean and matched-hour value differing by 1.6x.
3. LEVEL SELECTION. Ours selects interior interfaces by z_interface_ref; NEMO by depthw. Different level counts in the same band would bias a median.
4. Does our snapshot's saved K reflect a partial-cell/z-star thickness the oracle's does not?
5. Is the 38 m vs 71 m cross-check circular, i.e. does it use the same array as the ratio it is supposed to corroborate?
